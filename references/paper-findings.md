# Evidence behind Learn with AI

Source: Judy Hanwen Shen and Alex Tamkin, [*How AI Impacts Skill Formation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20245), arXiv:2601.20245v2, February 2026.

## What the study tested

- Randomized experiment with 52 professional or freelance Python programmers, 26 per group, who had not used Trio. They completed two short Trio coding tasks, with instructions and web search available to both groups; one group also had a GPT-4o coding assistant. Everyone then took a 27-point quiz without AI.
- The quiz targeted conceptual understanding, code reading, and debugging. It did not test long-term retention or broad professional competence.
- The AI group scored about 17 percentage points lower on the quiz (4.15 points out of 27; p = 0.010). The difference in average task completion time was not statistically significant (p = 0.391). See paper §§4–5, especially Figure 6.
- From screen recordings, the authors described six AI-use patterns. Conceptual inquiry, code plus explanation, and generation followed by comprehension had higher average quiz scores than pure delegation, progressive reliance, and repeated AI-led debugging. Each cluster was small (n = 2–7), and people were not randomized to patterns. See §6 and Figure 11.
- The authors suggest that encountering and independently resolving errors may support learning. This is an interpretation of observed behavior, not a separate causal test.

## What to carry into practice

Use AI to explain concepts, give targeted hints, and inspect generated work. Preserve opportunities for the learner to reason, debug, and demonstrate transfer without assistance. Offer full generation when needed, then use explanation and an independent check to support understanding.

## Limits

The study covered one library, a chat assistant, tasks under 35 minutes, and an immediate quiz. Its findings do not establish effects over months or years, across all domains, or for agentic tools. The interaction clusters are useful design cues, not proof that one prompt style causes a particular score.
